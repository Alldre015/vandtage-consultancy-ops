# Non-CRE Revenue Systems Outreach Test

**Status:** ACTIVE TEST
**Market wedge:** Non-CRE Revenue Systems
**Founder-attention allocation:** Approximately 30%
**Authority:** Allocation remains provisional and changes only through Founder ruling informed by human market evidence.

## Test objective

Determine whether high-value operator-led businesses outside CRE will confirm and pay to address a measurable revenue leak caused by weak intake, response, qualification, follow-up, routing, or visibility.

This test is designed to find evidence, not to select a permanent vertical in advance.

## Working hypothesis

An operator-led business is a plausible fit when:

- a qualified opportunity has enough value that missed follow-up matters;
- opportunity handling repeats often enough to form a system;
- the owner or accountable operator can describe the present workflow;
- a specific failure point can be measured;
- the business can provide an approved access path for implementation; and
- a credible outcome could support the active Founding Systems Partner price test.

The hypothesis is rejected or narrowed when human conversations show the problem is not important, not frequent, already solved, inaccessible, uneconomic, or owned by someone the outreach cannot reach.

## Allocation and batch rule

Maintain the current **approximately 70% CRE / 30% non-CRE** founder-attention allocation. A practical outreach batch is about seven CRE prospects and three non-CRE prospects for every ten net-new prospects, while allowing normal rounding.

The split measures founder attention, not just list size. Research, outreach, conversations, follow-up, and proposal work all count. Do not let a convenient non-CRE list silently displace the CRE priority.

## Initial cohort design

Select non-CRE prospects from operator-led businesses with an observable high-value opportunity flow. The first cohort should test more than one plausible business type rather than assume a winner.

For each prospect, record a supportable reason for selection:

- what valuable opportunity enters the business;
- where intake or follow-up may plausibly fragment;
- who appears accountable for revenue operations; and
- why the active price test could be economically rational if the problem is confirmed.

Do not infer an internal failure from public information. Public signals justify a question, not a claim.

## Core outreach message

**Subject:** A question about how new opportunities get handled

> Hi [First name],
>
> I’m testing a Vandtage engagement for operator-led businesses where valuable inquiries or referrals can lose momentum between intake, qualification, follow-up, routing, and a clear next decision.
>
> I’m not assuming that is happening at [Company]. I’m speaking directly with operators to learn where the workflow creates real revenue drag and where it is already working well.
>
> Would you be open to a 15–20 minute conversation about how a qualified opportunity moves through your business today? If there is a measurable bottleneck, I can outline what a bounded 90-day systems engagement would need to prove before either of us treats it as a fit.
>
> Andre

## Concise direct-message variant

> [First name] — quick operator question: when a valuable inquiry or referral comes in, is intake → qualification → follow-up → ownership already tight, or is there manual drag between those steps? I’m testing a Vandtage systems engagement around that workflow and would value a candid 15-minute perspective.

## Follow-up

> Hi [First name] — one follow-up on the narrow question below. I’m trying to learn whether opportunity-handling friction is commercially important for operator-led businesses, not assume that it is. A quick “not a problem here” is useful evidence; if it is a live issue, I’d welcome 15–20 minutes to understand where it breaks.

## Evidence to capture

Each human response or conversation should capture:

- business type and accountable role;
- whether the proposed problem was confirmed, rejected, or reframed;
- the specific workflow step creating drag;
- the human's description of frequency or impact;
- present workaround and ownership;
- urgency and willingness to change;
- commercial reaction to a bounded managed engagement, when appropriate;
- reason for no fit or objection; and
- evidence reference, next action, owner, and date.

## Advancement rule

Human commercial evidence governs every decision to repeat, narrow, expand, reposition, or stop the test.

- A sent message proves only that outreach occurred.
- A reply proves only what the person actually said.
- A booked call proves willingness to discuss, not problem severity or buying intent.
- A confirmed bottleneck requires the human's description of the workflow and impact.
- A qualified opportunity requires a real problem plus authority, urgency, economic plausibility, and an acceptable access path.
- A proposal requires a scoped requirement, technical feasibility check, priced scope, and Founder approval.
- A won engagement requires signed authorization.

Demo completion, internal confidence, engagement metrics, and agent inference do not substitute for human evidence.

## Review decision

At the end of each outreach batch, compare CRE and non-CRE evidence without treating silence as proof of market rejection. Andre decides whether to preserve the 70/30 allocation, change the tested segment or message, authorize another batch, or change the allocation through a new Founder ruling.

Keep only generalized or anonymized learning in this repository. Live prospect identity and confidential interaction detail remain in the approved operating system of record.
