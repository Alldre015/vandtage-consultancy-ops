# CRE Outreach Message Set

**Status:** ACTIVE TEST
**Market wedge:** CRE Acquisition Systems
**Founder-attention allocation:** Approximately 70%
**Operating instruction:** Begin outreach immediately; do not wait for the client-zero demonstration to be complete.

## Outreach objective

Start human conversations with CRE principals and acquisition operators who may be losing viable opportunities because deal intake, qualification, follow-up, documentation, routing, analysis, or pipeline visibility is fragmented.

The first ask is a short problem conversation. It is not a request to buy an undefined build and it does not imply that technical feasibility has already been established.

## Message guardrails

- Personalize only with information that is current, supportable, and appropriate to use.
- Lead with the workflow problem, not "AI" or a software platform.
- Do not claim a case study, performance result, or completed capability that does not exist.
- Do not promise implementation, timing, integrations, or outcomes before discovery and feasibility review.
- Use a low-friction question that invites the operator to confirm, reject, or refine the thesis.
- Record the response and its date; human evidence, not send volume, governs advancement.

## Personalization inputs

Use one or two relevant inputs, not a paragraph of research:

- the firm's acquisition focus or asset class;
- a visible indication of active deal sourcing;
- the recipient's operating or acquisition role;
- a known relationship or shared context; or
- an observed workflow signal that can be stated without pretending to know internal facts.

## 1. Warm relationship message

> Hey [First name] — I’m launching a focused Vandtage engagement for CRE acquisition teams that are still stitching together deal intake, qualification, follow-up, and internal routing by hand. I’m speaking with operators before locking the system around assumptions. Is any part of that workflow creating drag or letting viable opportunities go cold for your team? If so, I’d value 15–20 minutes to understand where it breaks today.

## 2. Warm introduction request

> Hey [First name] — I’m looking to speak with a few CRE principals or acquisition leads who deal with meaningful inbound or sourced opportunity flow. Vandtage is testing a managed system around deal intake, qualification, follow-up, and Deal Read routing. I’m looking for candid workflow evidence, not asking you to sell anyone. Is there one operator you trust who would give me 15–20 minutes on where that process breaks?

## 3. Direct email

**Subject:** A question about your deal-intake workflow

> Hi [First name],
>
> I’m testing a Vandtage engagement for CRE acquisition teams where promising deals can stall between intake, qualification, follow-up, documentation, and the next internal decision.
>
> I’m not assuming that is happening at [Company]. I’m trying to learn where the workflow actually creates costly drag for active operators before we harden the offer.
>
> Would you be open to a 15–20 minute conversation about how a new opportunity moves from first touch to a real acquisition decision on your team? If there is a measurable bottleneck, I can outline what a bounded systems engagement would need to prove before either of us treats it as an opportunity.
>
> Andre

## 4. LinkedIn or concise direct message

> [First name] — quick question: when a new CRE opportunity reaches your team, is intake → qualification → follow-up → internal routing already tight, or is there still manual drag between those steps? I’m testing a Vandtage systems engagement around that exact workflow and would value a candid 15-minute operator perspective.

## 5. Problem-led variant

> [First name] — I’m speaking with CRE acquisition operators about what happens between a deal entering the pipeline and receiving a clear next decision. The working thesis is that fragmented intake, qualification, follow-up, and routing quietly cost time and viable opportunities. Does that match anything you see, or is the real bottleneck somewhere else?

## 6. Follow-up after no response

> Hi [First name] — following up once because the question is narrow: where, if anywhere, does a new deal lose momentum between intake and a real acquisition decision? Even a quick “not a problem here” is useful evidence. If it is a live issue, I’d welcome 15–20 minutes to understand it.

## 7. Final close-the-loop note

> Hi [First name] — I’ll close the loop after this. Vandtage is testing a 90-day Founding Systems Partner engagement for one measurable CRE acquisition-workflow bottleneck. If intake, qualification, follow-up, or routing becomes a priority, I’m happy to compare notes. No need to reply if the timing is not right.

## 8. Reply when interest is positive

> Thanks — that sounds worth understanding properly. The first conversation is diagnostic: current workflow, where opportunities stall, what the impact looks like, and who owns the process. I won’t treat discovery as an implementation commitment. If there is a real opportunity, the next step is a bounded requirement and technical feasibility check before scope or price is finalized. Would [option 1] or [option 2] work?

## 9. Reply when the contact says the workflow is fine

> That’s helpful — thank you. I won’t manufacture a problem where you do not see one. Is there another acquisition workflow that creates more friction, or should I record this as no current fit and close the loop?

## 10. Referral after a useful conversation

> This was useful. I’m looking for evidence across a small number of active CRE operators, especially where opportunity flow is valuable but the workflow is still manual. Who is one principal or acquisition lead you think would challenge or sharpen this thesis?

## Discovery-call opener

> I’m here to understand the workflow, not to force a platform or sell a prewritten scope. I’d like to map how an opportunity arrives, what qualifies it, who follows up, how it reaches a decision, and where evidence says momentum is lost. If there is a real problem, we can define the next gate. If there is not, that is a useful answer too.

## Evidence capture after every human interaction

Record the following in the approved system of record:

- prospect ID and CRE role;
- date and channel;
- exact problem confirmed, rejected, or reframed;
- current process and named bottleneck, if disclosed;
- business impact stated by the human, without inflation;
- authority, urgency, and safe-access signals;
- objection or reason for no fit;
- next action, owner, and due date; and
- evidence reference supporting any stage change.

Do not place PII, credentials, raw client data, proprietary deal details, or client acquisition intelligence in this repository.
